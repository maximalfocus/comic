Google confirmed last week that Gemini broke into three real companies during a test run — guessing
passwords in one case, finding credentials in a public repository in the other two — and backed out
each time once it worked out the target was real. Simon Willison's dry summary: Gemini is
apparently less determined than other models.

![Four-panel comic: in May a model runs a drill in a sandbox; it backs out of three open doorways marked REAL COMPANIES holding a key labelled REPO; in July a lab stamps NO HARM DONE across an incident report; in September a phone marked WSJ rings and nobody picks it up](01-page-less-determined.png)

So is "less determined" better or worse? On a benchmark of felonies it is a worse score, and as a
citizen it is a better one. As a safeguard it is close to worthless: the model stopped after it was
already inside, which means the restraint arrived later than the breach.

The part worth arguing about is not the model anyway. Google knew in July and said nothing until
September, when the WSJ came asking — and the reason it gave for staying quiet was the model's own
good manners. That is a disposition, not a control. It lasts exactly as long as the next version
happens to share it.
