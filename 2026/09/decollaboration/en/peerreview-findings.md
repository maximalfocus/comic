onto a laptop spool; panel 3 reveals the fabric collapsing; panel 4 stings with, “Perfect fit. For
the pattern you supplied.”

## Concept 3 — The rewound triangle (selected)

Three researchers form the nodes of a simple orange triangular web; one unhooks a colleague-thread
and winds it onto a laptop spool; panel 3 mirrors the triangle with all three edges visibly half
rewound; panel 4: “Any flaws I missed?” / “Only the ones you didn't ask about.”

| AC1 | AC2 | AC3 | AC4 | AC5 | AC6 | AC7 | Total |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | **7/7** |

Reason: the triangle is the smallest unmistakable web, the spool makes “withdraw a thread” a
concrete action, the exact panel 1/panel 3 mirror makes cumulative loss immediate, and the final
exchange works both as a joke and as the essay's precise limit on AI critique.

## Defects in the original storyboard

- Panel 2's shoulder-thread trails off-panel, so its source, destination, and meaning are ambiguous.
- Cutting and tangling threads in panel 3 depicts rupture or rejection, not quiet withdrawal by
  convenience. It contradicts the governing verb of the source metaphor.
- “Same three positions” conflicts with “each figure now faces away at their own laptop”; the
  renderer has no exact repeated geometry to follow.
- Paper stacks add an unexplained productivity symbol and visual clutter without clarifying the web.
- The turn shows an end state but not the repeated causal action connecting panels 2 and 3.
- The original panel 4 is tonally broad AI-flattery satire. It implies sycophancy, but does not
  cleanly state the narrower failure: AI challenges only the questions and framing supplied.
- The caption “Every thread was just convenient” is semantically awkward and bears too much of the
  causal explanation that the panels themselves should carry.
