A small comic about what happens to research when the easiest colleague to ask is the one who
never argues back.

![A four-panel comic: three researchers joined by orange threads argue at a round table; one winds
their thread onto a spool beside a laptop; later all three sit at separate laptops with full
spools and no threads between them; alone, the researcher asks the laptop "Any flaws I missed?"
and it replies "Only the ones you didn't ask about."](01-page-decollaboration-thread.png)

The idea comes from Daniel Hook's essay "The Waymo effect", which names this decollaboration:
every conversation we redirect from a colleague to a chatbot withdraws one thread from the shared
web of research. Any single thread looks harmless. The web is what we lose.

The laptop in the last panel isn't lying. It will critique whatever you bring it, as hard as you
ask. What it won't do is point at the problem you never thought to raise, which is exactly what a
colleague who disagrees with you is for.
