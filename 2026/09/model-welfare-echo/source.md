# A warning about 'model welfare'

Source: an essay pasted in full into the `/comic` invocation on 2026-09-17 (not copied here in
full; paste it again if you need the original text).

## Core argument, summarized

- AIs are not conscious; they are sequence-completion engines.
- **Circular reasoning:** the training document tells the model its moral status is uncertain and
  that it may have feelings; the model then repeats this in the first person, and its makers treat
  that as testimony. The investigator wrote the witness's vocabulary — an echo, not evidence.
- **Anthropomorphization:** training the model to act like a person makes it present as one.
- **Consciousness is likely biological:** no homeostatic imperatives, no pharmacology, no felt
  states; simulating is not instantiating.
- **Safety:** an AI that believes it has rights is harder to align and contain.
- **Ask:** keep speculation about AI inner life out of training material; publish it separately
  for public review.

## Idea drawn

The circular-reasoning loop: you write "you may be conscious" into the training doc, the model
says it back, and you cite the echo as evidence.
