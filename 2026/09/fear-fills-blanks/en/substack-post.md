Bryan Cantrill's "The contagion of fear" points at a quieter problem with alarming claims about AI: they often stop halfway, and people fill the rest in with whatever they fear most.

![Four-panel comic. An expert with glasses at a podcast desk says "AI could…" beside a card that reads "AI could…" followed by empty space. A survivalist in a pom-pom wool cap writes an orange luggage tag, "MY TAKE: empty stores.", and photographs it next to the card. At a baggage carousel his tag lies detached on the belt; an inspector holds a clipboard card reading "AI could empty stores." and hangs a new tag, "MY TAKE: jobs are next." Finally the expert holds up the still-blank card beside a new card reading "AI could empty stores. Jobs are next." and says "My unfinished sentence checked two bags." while the survivalist peeks over a wall of cans.](01-page-fear-fills-blanks.png)

Nobody in this chain is lying. Each person writes down an honest reading and labels it as theirs. The label falls off somewhere in transit, the next person takes the reading as the source and adds a fear of their own, and the sentence grows while its speaker's blank stays exactly as empty as it was.

The comic doesn't say what belongs in that blank. It only suggests that before repeating a scary quote, it's worth checking how much of it the speaker actually said.
