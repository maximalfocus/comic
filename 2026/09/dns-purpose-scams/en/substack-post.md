Terence Eden argues that, judged by what it actually does, the Domain Name System looks like a machine for running scams. I drew his argument as four panels.

![A four-panel stick-figure comic. 1: a registrar clerk hands a baker a domain tag, "Live worldwide in one minute!" 2: a queue of sunglasses-wearing scammers collects tags such as gov.uk-dwpaph.bond, "Next! …Next! …Next!" 3: the clerk stares at a board reading "2025: 85M new gTLD registrations · 10%+ blocklisted", "Maybe 1 in 5 are scams. What are we FOR?" 4: the baker waits outside a gate with ID, money and calendar icons while a scammer eyes another registrar, "ID, €900 deposit, Monday… would scammers just shop elsewhere?"](01-page-dns-purpose-scams.png "The purpose of a system is what it does.")

The numbers come from an Interisle report, via Andrew Campling. Of roughly 85 million new gTLD registrations in 2025, at least 10% ended up on blocklists, and the report estimates that the share registered by malicious actors may be closer to 20%. Its methodology is disputed, but even the floor is grim.

The hard part is the last panel. Identity checks, deposits and waiting periods all land on honest registrants first, and scammers can move to whichever registrar is laxest. Eden doesn't claim to know the answer. He hopes DNS can become less dangerous while staying open.
